# Design Direction

Name the product or subject, its audience, and the page's main job before
choosing a visual direction. Derive visual language from the subject's real
materials, tools, artifacts, and vocabulary.

- Give the page one coherent identity rather than a collection of effects.
- Make the opening communicate the page's thesis through its strongest real
  content.
- Pair display, body, and utility typography deliberately. Use type hierarchy
  as part of the identity, not as neutral formatting.
- Use numbering, dividers, labels, and other structural devices only when they
  encode a real sequence, category, or relationship.
- Choose one memorable signature element. Keep surrounding elements quieter so
  it remains legible.
- Use motion when it clarifies hierarchy, continuity, or response. Prefer one
  orchestrated moment to unrelated effects.
- Match complexity to the direction. Maximal work needs disciplined execution;
  minimal work needs exact spacing, typography, and detail.

When the project already has a design system, make distinctive choices within
its vocabulary before inventing a second one.
